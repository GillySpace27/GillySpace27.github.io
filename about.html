About

Dr. Gilly

Research Scientist · NorthWest Research Associates · Boulder, Colorado

I study the Sun's corona and the wind it drives across the solar system: how the corona is heated, how waves carry energy through it, and how the solar wind gets up to speed. My work pairs forward models with observations from imagers, spectrometers, spectropolarimeters and coronagraphs, and I'm a Co-Investigator on NASA's PUNCH mission.

I earned my PhD at the University of Colorado Boulder with Steve Cranmer. Along the way I build open tools for the field, like the RHEF filter in SunPy's sunkit-image and the HelioSoftware apps.

About this site

This site is hand-built and deliberately simple: static HTML, one CSS file, a little vanilla JavaScript, and a single Cloudflare Worker (the ensō impressions endpoint). No npm, no bundler, no framework — every page is something you could open and read. Deployment is just git push.

The look comes from the site's own toys. The ensō calendar and editor settled on a warm-paper-and-ink palette with a single gold accent and a flash-free dark mode; the whole site now shares those exact design tokens. A small detail I'm fond of: the calendar and the editor reproduce the same daily ensō from the same date, byte for byte, by pulling the same pseudo-random draws in the same order — a determinism contract that lets the "edit this day" button round-trip perfectly.

The visual foundations began life as the HTML5 UP “Strata” template, used and adapted under CC BY 3.0. See also the archive of older pages.
